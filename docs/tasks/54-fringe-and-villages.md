# 54 The fringe, the suburbs and the villages

Wave 16, the third of the audit's plan (`52-roads-and-towns-audit.md`, items C4, C3, E1, A3 and F2), on its own branch (`wave16/fringe`, from `wave16/city-fabric`). Owns what lies between a city's last block and the fields (`c.fringe`, built in `fringe.js`), what a suburb looks like at every zoom and how its closes are driven, the plan of a village (`village.js`, `v.plan`) and what grows along an airport's access road (`landside.js`).

## What the owner said

> "nice natural looking streets and cities with depth and ... a good amount of variety in the cities from roads, buildings, highway interchanges etc. including access roads to airports"

## What was wrong

A city was an island on the field quilt: the blocks stopped on a line and the fields began at once (C4). Its suburbs read as empty at the town zoom, a thin street net over green with a few tiny houses, and the loops drawn inside the cul-de-sac blocks were a picture nothing drove (C3). A village was thirty boxes thrown either side of its road, no plots, no lane, no green, no church (E1, A3); the fields ran through it and the forest was painted over it. Nothing grew round an airport (F2).

## What this brief does

1. **Villages have a plan** (`IC.buildVillage(W, v, R, segs)` in `village.js`, kept in `v.plan`; `v.pop` is in hundreds). Three forms by size and chance: a *street village* (houses on plots fronting the road on both sides, a farm with its yard at each end), a *green village* (a green at the road with the houses and the church round it, the street running on), a *hamlet* (a few houses at the road). The street is the road through the village, sized to its people (300 m to 1.4 km), and runs on past the road's end as a lane where the road stops short; where no road reaches the village the whole street is a lane, marked `loose`, kept for the picture and left out of the drive graph. Lanes fork off the street (`W.lanes`, so the traffic drives them) until the houses match the people (five a hundred), with hedged gardens behind the houses (`v.plan.plots`, painted warm in the tiles from the region zoom, built ground so no fields there), a church with its yard in the bigger ones (`v.plan.church`), farms on the lanes outside. No house stands on a road (the roads as the cities left them: `indexSegs` again before the villages) or in the water. A clearing in the forest round every village (`W.forestD`, with a flat grid of flags so the cities' growth does not pay for it).
2. **Suburbs read as suburbs** (C3). A suburb block of the `cul` form gets its closes as streets of the city (`IC.culPlan(b)`, `IC.closeStreets(c, blocks, roads)` in `cities.js`): a way in from the block's street, a loop and closes ending in turning heads, or a single close for a small block (`b.close`). They are a tile layer of their own (`'cl'` in `terrain.js`, thinner than a street, heads only from the street zoom), drawn over the block's picture, and the traffic drives them: their links come last in the drive graph (`T.nMain`), so the town zoom skips them when the view is wider than 15 km; they carry a quarter of a street's floor (their own residents); their ends, drawn on the ellipse rather than on the loop's chords, are tied onto the loop and the street. At the town zoom (lod 2) a suburb block is rows of roofs on warm ground (`roofRows`), so the outer town reads as houses.
3. **The fringe** (C4, `IC.buildFringe(W, c, R, segs, villages)` in `fringe.js`, kept in `c.fringe`). Ribbon development along each road out of town (from a gate, or from a junction just outside it where a motorway rings the town): house plots fronting the road for 500–1,500 m beyond the last block, nine in ten at first and one in three at the end, with a petrol station, a car dealer or a garden centre among them. An outer ring in the open ground round the town, most of it off a road out past the ribbon, the rest anywhere near a road: allotments, playing fields, a cemetery, a sewage works by the river, a water tower on high ground, a scrapyard, riding stables (`IC.FRINGE_ITEMS`, `c.fringe.items`). A retail park (sheds round a car park) beside the busiest road out of a big city, past the ribbon. One or two outlying estates (`f: 'cul'` blocks turned to face their road, closed like the suburbs' with the way in from the road). Fringe items are painted in the tiles from the region zoom (`fringeItem`) and marked as built ground; the houses among them are blocks of the city (`d: 'sub'`, `b.fr`), so they count for traffic and can be hit.
4. **Along the access road** (F2, `IC.aptOuter(S, ap, L)` in `landside.js`, from `IC.landsideTick`): an airport's access road gathers a petrol station and a hotel at 2,000 passengers a day, a logistics park at 6,000 and a business park at 15,000, one at a time, each at its own place along the road and either side of it, the whole road scanned when that place is taken (a road that runs through the town has its free ground elsewhere). They pay a small income like the rest of the landside (`IC.LAND` with `outer: true`) and the map draws them (`render-airport.js`).

## Worth knowing

- Traffic (`traffic.js`): the vehicles on their own trips are filled only along the part of a link inside the view, so a motorway running through the view no longer takes most of the room (`AG_MAX`, now 3,000) for vehicles nobody sees and loses them when the view moves: the count jumped while zooming. The links in view come from a 64-unit grid (`linkGrid`). A street's end that stops a hair short of the street it meets is tied onto it (the crossing test is exact to a ten-thousandth of a piece).
- Generation: a last islands pass after the junctions trim the roads drops any street left hanging (`IC.cityDropIslands`, leaving the closes be); the budget is 11 s, brief 54 costing about 0.6 s on the owner's machine.

## Done when

## What it does not do

Buildings with depth, pavements and parked cars (55); junction footprints and the linear city's spine (56); the countryside's texture and the enemy's towns (57). A street village's houses stand on plots but the hedges are not drawn yet (55).
