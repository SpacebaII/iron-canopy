# 65 Towns from real data, and buildings with height

On its own branch, stacked on brief 57 (`wave16/countryside`). Owns the real samples (`tools/city-sample.js`, `tools/cities/`, `city-samples.js`, `city-cores.js`), the old towns laid on real ones (`realBlocks` in `cities.js`), the buildings standing up on the map (`IC.blockPrisms` in `buildings.js`, `render-buildings.js`) and the traffic lights (`IC.signalFor` in `traffic.js`).

## What the owner said

> "can we refine city generation by sampling real data as much as possible also if plausible add buildings with height into the world like the 3d view so it's not so flat and urban density can be felt ... I do really like our cities but they definitely have their downsides with how traffic looks performance and logical intersections"

## What was wrong

The generator's numbers were guesses. A city street was drawn 34 m wide and an avenue 40 m, two to three times a real one, so a quarter of every town was asphalt and the blocks sat far apart. Floors were picked from ranges nobody had checked, the same at the edge of town as in the middle. The old towns were rings of streets round the centre, a spider's web no real town has, with green left over between them. From above, a downtown of towers looked as flat as a suburb: only a shadow said a building was tall. Close in, the traffic cost most of a frame, and the avenues' crossings had no lights: cars crossed each other's paths at full flow.

## What this brief does

1. **Real towns measured** (`tools/city-sample.js`, places in `tools/cities/config.js`). The tool fetches patches of 36 real neighbourhoods from OpenStreetMap, ten kinds of fabric (old towns, nineteenth-century courtyard blocks, downtowns, terraces, houses on a grid, cul-de-sacs, post-war estates, business parks, works and warehouses), and measures each: junctions a km², the share of T junctions and dead ends, how ordered the streets are (orientation entropy), the spacing of the two street families, building coverage, buildings a hectare, plot depth, floors weighted by footprint, floor area ratio, and the corridor from building line to building line across main and minor streets. Four transects (Paris, Chicago, Warsaw, Madrid, from the centre out to 17 km) give how density falls away from the middle. The results are baked into `city-samples.js` (`IC.CITY_SAMPLES`), so the game never needs the network. `node tools/city-sample.js --print` prints the table.
2. **The generator uses them** (`cityfabric.js`: `IC.REAL_FABRIC`, `IC.realFloors`, `IC.realGrad`). Streets are 11 m and avenues 20 m (`IC.ROAD_SPEC`), with the building line set back as far as in the real towns (23–24 m between building lines); the old town's and the business district's street spacing are the measured ones. Floors come from the real samples of each form (an old town's 3–6, courtyard blocks 5–6, downtown towers 7–67 with the median at 14, estates' slabs 6–13), lower away from the centre by the measured gradient. A town has about a fifth more blocks than before, on the same ground.
3. **Old towns taken whole** (`IC.CITY_CORES`, `realBlocks` in `cities.js`). Five real old towns (Bologna, Nuremberg, Bruges, Kraków, Lucca) are stored as their streets, lanes and every building footprint (from the OpenStreetMap vector tiles at zoom 14, about 600 KB packed as text). A European town's old town is laid on one of them, picked by its name, turned and mirrored, clipped to its walls: the real streets become its streets (the traffic drives them), the lanes its footpaths, and each face of its streets with buildings in it a block of its own shape (`b.pg`) holding those buildings (`b.rb`, form `real`). The map paints them by their outlines; the 3D view stands each up as its smallest enclosing rectangle.
4. **Buildings standing up on the map** (`IC.blockPrisms`, `render-buildings.js`). Close in (from about 6 px a 100 m), every building of a planned block is drawn again over the traffic as a prism leaning back to the north by `IC.LEAN` (0.6) of its height: its south-facing walls, shaded by the sun in the north-west, plastered under tiled roofs, glass or concrete under flat ones; its roof, with the shaded slope of a pitched roof or a plant room on a tall flat one; window bands on every floor of a tall building close in; lit windows at night. Buildings in front hide the street behind them, so the traffic runs between them. They are painted into tiles of their own at the terrain's two closest levels, a few a frame, and repainted when the world changes (`dirtyBox`, `IC.bakeBlock`) or night falls (five steps).
5. **Traffic drawn faster.** A batch of vehicles was one path of thousands of closed outlines; in Chrome each `closePath` on such a path costs more the longer it is. Without it (the fill closes every outline) a frame over the capital close in drops from about 170 ms to 65 ms in the software renderer this was measured with.
6. **Traffic lights** (`IC.SIGNAL`, `IC.signalFor`). Junctions where avenues cross, and where a town's avenue crosses a street at a crossroads, have lights: the biggest road's axis, then the one across it, in a 64 s cycle (the avenue's share 60% where a street crosses it), 3 s of amber and 1 s of red both ways between. Close in, vehicles stop at the line 11 m short of the junction's middle on red, and on amber unless they are too close to stop; queues form and clear by turn. The heads stand at the kerb by each stop line, glowing at night.

## Worth knowing

- `tools/city-sample.js` keeps what it fetched in `tools/cities/raw/` (not committed). Node's own fetch needs `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1` behind a proxy. Overpass is not used; the patches come from the map API (`/api/0.6/map`) and the old towns from the vector tiles (`vector.openstreetmap.org/shortbread_v1`), decoded by `tools/cities/mvt.js`.
- The real data changes the generator's dice: every town on every seed is a different draw from before.
- Generation is about 12% slower on the machine that measured this (more blocks, the old towns' buildings); the budget test still passes.
- The tiles of standing buildings are drawn after the traffic and before the airports and units. Cloud shadows and the night tint are drawn before them, so their night is baked into the tiles.
- Not done: roofs and walls are not textured; buildings of the fringe and the estates' own pictures (cul-de-sacs, plots, petrol stations) stay flat; the lights do not show in the middle zoom's slots.

## Done when

- `npm test` green, with tests that the samples are loaded and used (street widths, setbacks, floors, the gradient) and that every old town laid on a real one is 20–95% built over with every block holding buildings (`world: towns are measured from real ones...`), that a town stands up with towers taller than houses, every building inside its block, none on a destroyed block and the old town's real outlines kept (`buildings: a town stands up on the map...`), and that the roads across each other at a junction with lights are never green at once, every road in gets green, vehicles wait at red and hardly any drive through it (`traffic: lights at the avenues' crossings...`).
