# Iron Canopy: guide for working on this project

Iron Canopy is a browser game. The player starts as Director of Civil Aviation, builds real-scale airports and runs fictional airlines, and rises through four acts to Chief of the Air Force while a neighbouring country escalates from gray-zone incidents to open war. The owner is building it for their portfolio and wants depth that stays readable: realistic systems, explained in plain words, that punish bad decisions fairly.

Task briefs for larger pieces of work are in `docs/tasks/`. Read the brief you were given, then this file.

## Run it

- Play: open `iron-canopy/index.html` in a browser, or `python devserver.py` and go to http://localhost:8766/iron-canopy/index.html.
- Test: `npm test` (about 7 minutes on 4 cores; the tests run in parallel, one worker per core), `npm run test:quick` (under 3 minutes: it skips the slow ones), `node tests/run.js <words>` for the tests whose name contains them. `npm run test:slow` is the long balance runs (the scripted Career through Act I, the Quick war commander), which GitHub runs every night; GitHub runs the rest in two halves on every push. Every test starts from seeded random numbers (`IC.seedRandom`), so a failure repeats when you run that test alone. A test that times the code (group `'alone'`) is timed again alone if it fails beside the others. Listeners a test adds with `IC.on` are removed when it ends; `IC.on` returns the function that removes one.
- See it (a game is built in stages while the page draws, so scripts `await IC.begin(mode)`): `node tools/shot.js <name> "<setup script>"` saves a full screenshot (UI included) to `shots/<name>.png` and reports page errors. It needs Playwright: `npm i --no-save playwright && npx playwright install chromium`. Open the PNG to look at it. Visual work is not done until you have looked at it.

## Rules

- Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No build step, no frameworks, no runtime dependencies. Scripts are classic scripts sharing one namespace, `window.IC`, loaded in the order listed in `iron-canopy/index.html`. The one exception is three.js, for the 3D replay window only (`replay3d.js`): it is loaded from cdnjs when the replay opens, and nothing else in the game or the tests depends on it.
- New game file? Add it to `index.html` in the right place and to the `FILES` list in `headless.js` (same order), or the tests will not load it.
- Keep the simulation free of DOM code. Everything in the step (`IC.step` in `sim.js`) must run headless in Node.
- Match the surrounding style: compact functions, short comments that say why, `const U = IC.U` helpers.
- Every change keeps `npm test` green. New systems get tests in `tests/run.js`: assert behaviour ("a departure takes off", "a crater shortens the runway"), not implementation details.
- The eight Academy lessons must stay completable (the suite plays them).
- Performance budget: a simulation step (0.25 game s) well under 1 ms on a mid-range laptop, rendering at 60 fps with a busy capital airport on screen. Measure after big changes (`tools/airspace-perf.js`: 300 flights and a raid; `tools/world-perf.js`: start-up and frames). Hot objects keep one shape: a field a loop over many of them reads is declared when they are made (see `IC.spawnThreat`), and the step's per-target radar work goes through the sensor grid in `IC.sense`.
- Work on a branch named for your task, commit in sensible steps, and open a pull request with a short summary of what changed, what you tested, and what you could not finish. Do not push to `main`.

## Words on screen

The player reads a lot of text: messages, event cards, tooltips, panel labels. Keep it plain and specific. Say what happened and what the player can do about it, in short sentences, with real units (km, min, ₭M). No jargon without a word of explanation, no invented terms the player has not been taught, no filler.

## Units and time

- 1 world unit = 100 m. The world is 57,000 × 43,000 units (5,700 × 4,300 km): the country is some 3,200 × 2,400 km, with 60 cities (170–400 km apart) and 300 villages. Airports are drawn at real size: a 3.4 km runway is 34 units long and 0.45 wide.
- Game seconds. `IC.GS = 10` game seconds per real second at 1×; speeds 1–32×, and skip (64×) stops when something needs the player.
- Two clocks. The live clock is `S.time` (aircraft, weather, building, day and night). The calendar counts months and years on top of it: a month is `IC.DAYS_PER_MONTH` = 3 live days (a game keeps its own in `S.cal.dpm`; tests pass `dpm` to `IC.newGame`, even a fraction of a day), Day 1 is 1 January, Year 1. `IC.MO(S, n)` is n months in game seconds, `IC.YR(S)` a year, `IC.calAt(S, t)` the date, `U.date` / `U.clock(t, S)` the words, `U.months` a duration in months. `IC.onMonth(fn)` runs at each month's turn (the statement, ageing). The Career paces itself by the calendar; a Quick war and the Academy stay on the live clock (`S.mode !== 'story'`), their dates show days.
- Waiting for money (`IC.waitStart`, `IC.waitTick` in the step, `IC.waitText`; `S.wait`): time runs at `IC.WAIT.speed` (432×, a month in about a minute) in 8 s steps while nothing hostile or armed is up, and stops when the treasury reaches what the player picked, the month turns, or anything that stops skip happens.
- Money is in ₭M (`U.money`). Distances `U.km`, durations `U.dur`.
- The camera zoom `IC.cam.z` is screen pixels per world unit, from about 0.02 (whole map) to `IC.MAXZ` = 320 (the vehicles round an aircraft at the gate). Anything drawn with a fixed world size must look right at both ends; effects scale with `WF` in `render.js`.

## How the airport model works

- An airport (`ap`, also in `S.infra`) has `parts` and `nodes`. Taxiways are polylines of node ids; a node that sits on a runway centreline or apron edge is linked to it (`node.on`). Taxiways can be one-way (`part.oneway` ±1) or carry a preferred flow (`part.flow`). `IC.aptGraph` builds the graph from built parts only (`G.adj`, reverse `G.radj`, runway node lists `G.rwn`, dependent-runway groups `G.grp`); aprons get stands laid out along their back edge (depth decides size S/M/L). Every part and stand has a zone (passenger, cargo, light aircraft, military): civil aircraft never park in the military zone and the reverse.
- Wind (`S.wind`: `dir` it blows from, `kt`, `gust`) and visibility (`IC.sky`, `IC.needILS`) come from `weather.js`. Aircraft types have crosswind and tailwind limits (`xw`, `tw`); `IC.rwWindBlock` says when a runway is closed to a type. `IC.aptConfig` picks a direction for each runway and a role (arrivals, departures, mixed, spare); in fog, arrivals need a landing system (`ils` part) on the end they land on.
- `IC.aptStats` measures what the airport can do (runway occupancy per configuration and traffic mix, arrivals and departures an hour, stands per zone, gates against remote stands, terminal capacity, fuel by trucks or hydrant, fire-truck response) and lists what is wrong in plain words (`st.warn`). The numbers use the same timings as the simulation (`IC.rwOcc`).
- Ground movement (`groundops.js`): runways that cross share one clearance (`ap.rl`, keyed by group); parallels closer than 760 m keep their own but are configured as a pair, departures on the longer and arrivals on the shorter. Aircraft stop at hold-short lines 75 m before a runway; aircraft at the same line cross together; a crossing starts only when the way off is clear. Routes are planned with time reservations (`ap.res`) so opposite traffic on a single taxiway is avoided in advance; an aircraft kept waiting by oncoming traffic claims the taxiway next. Only stopped, nose-to-nose traffic is a "gridlock" (one is towed clear, counted against the airport). Scrambles, then arrivals, have priority for runways.
- Airlines fly real aircraft between our airports and foreign ones. Arrivals get a runway from the arrival manager (`IC.gopsFaf`), ask for it at the final approach fix and hold if it is busy, its exit is blocked or no stand is free; long holds divert. Turnaround depends on contact stands, terminal load and fuel.
- Accidents are rare and always have a cause: gusts just beyond a crew's limit, a wet or short runway, an incursion at night or in fog where there are runway crossings and no ground radar, birds near water. Fire-truck response decides how many die; the wreck closes the runway until engineers clear it; an accident report card follows.
- Airport life (wave 7): buildings snap to the nearest taxiway or apron edge and get a taxiway to their door or a service road (`IC.bldSnapBuilding`); aprons that touch are one paved area, stretched with the Stretch tool, and take stands placed by hand (`apron.ramp`, `apron.free`: size, turn, nose-in or drive-through). The site grows with what is built (`IC.aptInSite`); units stand inside the fence off the pavement. On stands, `s.svc` says what serves the aircraft (jet bridge, walking, buses, lorries, fuel truck or hydrant); departures may stop at a fuel stand or de-icing pad (`m.via`), pushbacks and tows count as ground movements (`m.gmLog`, `ap.gmLog`); airliners go to a hangar for maintenance every dozen or so landings (`hangar.inside`). `IC.partNow` says what a building is doing; `IC.aptProvides(ap)` measures what the airport offers airlines. In the Career, research opens reinforced concrete, hydrant fuel, ground radar, CAT III landing systems and jet bridges (`IC.APT_TECH`, `IC.aptLockWhy`).
- Damage: craters split runways into strips (`IC.rwStrips`), taxiway segments get cut, buildings lose hit points, burning fuel spreads to tanks within 160 m, aircraft in the open die more easily than in shelters.

## How height, airspace and air traffic control work

- Every flying thing has `alt`, km above the ground. Aircraft show flight levels above 6,000 ft and feet below it; everything else km (`IC.altText`). Interceptors fly a climb-and-descend path to the target's height and hit only within `IC.HIT_R` in 3D: one passing 8 km above is spent ("PASSED ABOVE"). Each missile reaches as far as its table says at the target's height (`IC.REACH`): long-range rounds reach less than half as far against a sea-skimmer.
- Airspace: each of our airports gets the preset for its size (`field`, `regional`, `hub`, `base`) until the player changes it; big layouts stretch it so every final approach fix is inside the control zone. Volumes are rings with a floor and ceiling; airways are class A above FL195 and E below; the rest is G. Classes decide who needs a clearance (light aircraft stay out of A–D unless cleared, flying round zones from the ground up and under shelves), who is kept apart, and 250 kt below FL100. Military volumes (R, Q, X) close a height band: airliners are given a level above or below it.
- The editor (the airport's Airspace tab, `airspaceTab` in `inspector.js`; orders in `IC.aspAct`, `sideview.js`): with the tab open, a click inside a ring picks it and its edge drags on the map (`IC.aspResize` keeps the next ring touching); floors and ceilings drag in the tab's slice or the larger side view, in 500 ft steps; "Draw a shelf" makes a ring from the last one out to where the player clicks. Every class has a one-line meaning (`IC.ASP_CLS[c].brief`), every ring a sentence (`IC.aspWords`), every change a line in the log (`IC.aspShort`).
- Controllers work in sectors (tower, approach per airport, area sectors split by nearest centre). A flight's weight rises off radar, off airways, climbing, descending, holding and at each handover; an overloaded sector spaces departures wider and misses conflicts. Spacing is 1,000 ft or 5 NM under radar, 10 NM without; pairs are found on a 70 km grid.
- Arrivals 150 km out get a landing slot one runway gap (`IC.atcGap`, from the runways and the tower's rules) after the last; small delays are flown off by slowing down, bigger ones by a dog-leg, the rest in a holding stack 35 km out, 1,000 ft per level. Departures are kept under the arrivals until they leave the terminal area, then step-climb. Lanes: arrivals come by a gate 15 km out on the runway line, departures fly the runway heading 15 km first.

## How roads, towns and traffic work

- Roads are routed by A* on a 6 km cost grid (`buildNetwork` in `gen.js`): slopes, forest and river crossings cost more, and running on an existing road is cheap, so roads merge. Classes in `edge.cls`: `hw` motorway, `rd` main road, `lc` local road, `sp` access road. `W.nodes` are places plus junctions (`jct`, interchanges `ix`); `W.lanes` are rural lanes and `city.streets` city streets, both drawn and driven but not routed.
- Cities (`cities.js`): a city grows out from its centre over a cost field (roads cheap; hills, forest and rivers dear), so it reaches out along roads and valleys and swallows villages. Its street plan is a lattice bent by a smooth warp (`IC.cityFrame(c)`, parameters in `c.lat`); a style (`c.style`: `eu`, `us`, `new`, `ind`, and `east` for the enemy's towns) sets the plan, districts and buildings. Every block has a district `b.d` (`old`, `biz`, `dense`, `sub`, `ind`, `log`, `rail`), a building form `b.f`, its lattice cell `b.i, b.j`, and the old flags `core`, `ind`, `sub`. The city keeps `blocks`, `streets`, `parks`, `squares`, its reach `ext` and its district mix `mix`; `IC.cityDemand(c)` turns the mix into cargo, business and leisure demand and `IC.cityCharacter(c)` into words. `W.farmAt` is zero on built ground (`W.builtAt`).
- Junctions (`junctions` in `gen.js`): every road junction has a shape in `W.junctions` (`mm` cloverleaf, `mx` diamond, `rb` roundabout, `tj` kerbed junction) with slip roads in `W.ramps`; routing still runs through the node. Roads cross railways at `W.railX`.
- How they are drawn (brief 46, `roadgeom.js` then `render-roads.js`): roads are laid at their real cross-section, minor classes first, with butt ends; every junction is then one surface of asphalt laid over the roads' own markings, its corners curb returns (8 m in town, 12–20 m on country roads) tangent to both edges, and its own markings on top: the main road's centre line through, its edge line solid on the free side and broken across each mouth, the curb returns' edge lines, and a double give-way line across the lane coming in on each minor road. Roundabouts: ring, kerbed island with a lorry apron and planting, flared entries, splitter islands, give-way lines (vehicles inside the ring are drawn going round it). Slip roads: an auxiliary lane and a taper where they leave or join, a gore of chevrons where they part, a T with curb returns where they meet the road across. The road over an interchange has a deck from the junction out along each arm; a local or access road crosses a railway at a level crossing (panels, rails, stop lines, half barriers), anything bigger on a bridge; rails, sleepers and ballast are drawn live close in. City street crossings (`IC.paintStreetJoin`) are painted into the tiles after the blocks (and live over the avenues), rounded into the blocks' lots, with zebras, stop lines and close in lane arrows; the landside's roads get the same corners, a drop-off lane and zebras along the terminal. Far out, roads are pale greys by class (`IC.ROAD_TONE`), the same in the tiles and the live drawing.
- The map (`terrain.js`): a far image (colour × hillshade), a base image of the whole map (`IC.TS` px a unit), and tiles at five levels (level 0 is the base's picture with roads and towns sharp, for the regional zoom), painted on demand: fields in farm blocks lying along `W.fieldAng`, forest from the density `W.forestD`, airfield grass from the airport's parts, towns, and marks. Change world data, then call `IC.worldChanged(S, box)`; the map repaints that box.
- Damage (`state.js`): `IC.groundAt` says what a weapon landed on; a block takes the hit (`IC.blockHit`, `b.hp` 1 → 0), everything else gets a mark in `S.marks` (field and forest scorch fade in under two days, road craters stay until the road engineers repair the road (growth.js), then leave a patch). Tiles bake marks in and repaint as they fade.
- Radar and hills: every ground radar, civil or military, uses the terrain profile in `airspace.js` (`IC.aspHidden`, `IC.radarFloor`); the coverage layer shows military cover in blue with the holes behind hills.
- Traffic (`traffic.js`): roads, streets, slip roads and lanes form one drive graph (streets meet where they cross; motorways only by their one-way slip roads). City blocks are grouped into zones with homes, jobs and freight; commuters, lorries, airport travellers and errands are assigned to routes once (again when roads change), giving every link a flow per direction and purpose. Every two game minutes (and at once when an alert starts or ends) the step turns flows into loads for the hour (war, alerts, cut roads). In the middle zoom `IC.trafficVisible` places vehicles of each kind in slots riding the flow; from z 10 `IC.trafficAgents` (called by the renderer, never the step) runs vehicles near the camera that look like traffic without planning trips: they leave zones or come in on busy links, take each next road at random weighted by its load and how straight on it is, park after a few km, keep lane and distance, slow through junctions and give way to whoever is in one, and queue in front of a cut road before turning back. There are no signal phases. The middle zoom shows the queue in front of a cut road too (`T.cutN`). Close in (z ≥ 1.3) `render-roads.js` draws the network live at real width; tiles carry streets and lanes.

## How the enemy commander works

- An aim (`E.aim`: coerce the government, break morale, strangle trade, ground our air power; `IC.EAIMS`) weights seven target sets (`IC.ESETS`, candidates from `IC.enemyTargets`): cities and their power, roads and bridges and supply, airports and trade, fuel, air defence, air bases, command. It keeps an aim for days and switches after two failed raids in a row. Targets it keeps hitting, or where our defence keeps winning (`E.hard`), lose weight; before act 4 no set may take more than about a quarter of the fire, and the main air base (`IC.mainBase`) a fifth at any time.
- Four acts (`E.act`, `IC.EACTS`): probing (a few drones and missiles at targets a known battery covers, to see what fires), limited strikes on soft targets while it saves (`s.hold`, `s.want`, `s.saved` on the sites: ballistic missiles are not fired while saving; `IC.enemyStock` says how far it has come), the shock (four targets of different kinds at once: decoys first to empty a battery, an axis our radars do not watch, or something nobody guards; then a lull), and the campaign (`E.c4`: radars, then batteries it has seen fire a lot (`guessFill`), then supply, then the air base, with raids on the aim's other targets between).
- Pace (`IC.EPACE`): after each raid the defence is winning if it stopped 60% or the raid hit at most one thing and did not get what it came for; hours in that state add up in `E.winH`. The shock needs 8 and three limited strikes, act 4 needs 16 and at least 36 h of war; ceilings (16 h for act 1, 28 h of saving, 52 h for act 4) keep a weak defence from stalling the war. `IC.enemyOpening(S, { act, head, winH })` starts further along (the sandbox, a strong Career Act III, a Quick war defence that was ready).
- Real stocks: sites produce by `regen` scaled by their health; a hit burns stock, a lost launcher is replaced in `IC.TEL_REPLACE`, lost aircraft are gone. Intelligence reports the saving (`E.intel`, the Intel room) hours before the shock.
- Every raid is still a cycle: planned as soon as the last one is over, nothing launched for the first 90 minutes, an intelligence warning about an hour out, the raid, an after-action report that says what got through, why, and what they were after.

## How growth, trade and roads work

- Travel times are per place, not per car: `IC.travelFrom` runs Dijkstra over `W.edges` at class speeds (motorway 100 km/h, main 75, local 55); a cut road (`edge.cut`, a crater) or a fallen bridge is six times slower. `S.econ.tt` holds trees from every city and industry with cuts, `S.econ.ti` without. `IC.roadsChanged(S)` re-plans convoy routing and marks these stale.
- Demand: a city wants `pop × 28 × prosperity` flights a day (`IC.GROWTH`), times its air score: each airport within 2.5 h by road counts for its quality (frequency, places served, delays over the last day, fares). Airports' load factors (`ap.svc.lf`) fill the seats in `aviation.js`; airlines ask for routes where seats run full and not where they fly half empty.
- Industries (`S.econ.inds`, at remote villages): sales = capacity × (a floor, a home market by road, exports by air cargo or lorry over a neutral border). Trade taxes are 15% of sales and follow the story's tax share.
- Cities grow with their air score and road reach, shrink with war damage, drift down slowly with neither. Blocks follow population (`city.bpp` blocks per thousand): new ones fill cells of the city's own street plan next to built ones, pulled by roads, rail and airports, with a district and building, and streets on their open sides (`block.grown`); shrinking marks the outer ones `empty`. The district mix follows. Every change calls `IC.worldChanged`.
- Roads: a hit within reach of a road (`IC.roadHit`, from `IC.detonate`) lowers `edge.cond`; below 0.6 it is cut until engineers bring it back (about 10–15% an hour, three times that when rushed, `IC.rushRepair`). The player builds roads only for airports (`IC.ROADS`: access road, link road, motorway link, which ends on a motorway at a new interchange); `IC.roadPlan` refuses anything else and says why. They are works in `W.roadWorks` (surveying, earthworks, paving) and open as new edges, splitting existing roads where they join; bridges they need become real bridges. `IC.aptCatchment` counts the people within reach of an airport by road.
- Money: pay with `IC.pay(S, kind, amount)` so the payment is booked (`IC.econBook`) by kind (names in `IC.STATEMENT`) into the day's and the month's books; `IC.monthStatement(S, 0 | 1)` is this month or last month, `IC.yearReview(S, y)` a year's months with what changed, and anything not booked shows as "Building works and other spending". `IC.money(S)` is the hourly view (income and running costs, each line with its reason from `IC.moneyWhy`). Loans repay over their term: 1% a month in the Career (`IC.LOANS`), 0.4% a day on the live clock (`IC.LOANS_LIVE`).

## How saving works

- A save is the seed plus the state, written by one generic serializer (`save.js`); new fields in `S` are saved without code there. The world is regenerated from its seed: `IC.worldBase` (called by `IC.newGame` right after generation) gives every world object an id and a fingerprint, and a save keeps only the world objects that changed. A change to world generation changes the fingerprints, and older saves are refused with a message rather than loaded broken.
- Objects in the static tables (`IC.UNITS`, `IC.ACTYPES`, `IC.CHAPTERS`, any `IC.UPPER_CASE` table) and functions on `IC` are saved by name, so `u.d === IC.UNITS[u.type]` after a load. Shared objects stay shared. Maps, Sets and typed arrays are kept.
- Caches are skipped and rebuilt: keys starting with `_`, an airport's route trees, and the keys in `IC.SAVE_SKIP` (traffic, terrain, radar-cover grids, travel-time trees). A cache you add: start its key with `_`, or add it to `IC.SAVE_SKIP` and rebuild it in `IC.afterLoad`. Anything that holds a canvas is the renderer's and is not saved.
- A function kept in the state must be a named handler: `IC.hfn('name', ...args)` with a factory `IC.H.name = (...args) => function`, or `IC.later(S, dt, 'name', ...args)` for `S.later`. Objects with behaviour (goals, beats, event cards) are tagged with `IC.remake(obj, 'name', ...args)` and made again by `IC.REMAKE.name`; event cards are made by name (`EV` in `story.js`), with anything random picked by the caller. A closure in the state is dropped and listed in the save's `lost`, and the save tests fail.
- The save tests save a Career and a Quick war, load them, save again (both saves must match), and play both on with the same dice: they must keep step. `IC.loadSave(json)` in a test starts from a saved position instead of replaying.

## How supply works

- Buying a unit: `IC.deploy` places it from the reserve, or buys it if there is none (`IC.buyBlock` says why not). It loads for 2 min at the nearest depot, garrison, city barracks or airfield (`IC.deliveryPlan`) and drives there, or is flown from the nearest airfield by heavy-lift helicopter when the drive would take over 25 min (`IC.AIRLIFT`), then sets up. No production queue.
- Stock (missiles and rockets) lives in depots. Each depot's truck companies (3 lorries each) take it to units inside its ring, first-priority areas (`depot.pri`) and the emptiest units first; forward depots refill from the Central Depot. Stock is bought from the arms plants (`IC.buyStock`, by rail, `IC.SUPPLY.railKmh`) or imported by air when every plant is down. `S.supply.auto` (Keep stocked) buys whatever falls below half of one full reload, never below `S.supply.floor`.
- Convoys drive `IC.route(…, true)` (roads only) at `IC.SUPPLY.kmh` by class, slower in towns at rush hour, crawling past cuts; `v.cut` names the cut. `IC.nextLoad(S, u, mag)` is what a unit's panel says about its next load.

## Balance as it stands (change it deliberately)

- Airport parts cost per 100 m (runway ₭15M, taxiway ₭4M), per hectare (apron ₭12M, terminal ₭40M) or each (hangar ₭60M, hardened shelter ₭120M, fuel tank ₭35M, landing system ₭25M, ground radar ₭60M, hydrant system ₭90M). Those are concrete prices: asphalt ×0.7 (carries 90 t), grass ×0.15 (6 t), reinforced concrete ×1.6 (600 t, craters about half the size). Upkeep is 0.12% of cost per game hour.
- Construction: 10% of the cost must be in hand to start; the rest is paid stage by stage (survey 3%, earthworks 27%, paving 50%, markings and lights 20%). Paving needs about 10 lorry loads of concrete (8 of asphalt) a hectare; an industrial town delivers 20 + 1.5 × its industry loads an hour. Clearing a city block costs ₭6–40M in compensation and public support.
- Runway separation: 480 s without a tower, 110 s with one, 60 s with an approach radar (half that after an arrival). Each tank's fuel trucks refuel 8 aircraft an hour; a hydrant system removes the limit and pipes in 900 fuel units an hour.
- Landing fee is half the type's `fee`, passenger charge ₭0.0035M per passenger; both scale with the airport's charge level.
- Roads cost per km on flat ground ₭2M (access road), ₭5M (link road), ₭15M (motorway link, plus ₭40M for its interchange), plus ₭15M/₭40M/₭100M a bridge; hills and forest add up to 170%. They build at 4/2/1 km a game hour.
- A city grows up to 0.5% a day with perfect air service, 1.5% a day for each 100% better road reach than at the start, and drifts −0.08% a day with neither. In the Career it grows by the year (`IC.GROWTH.airYear` 7%, `roadYear` 20%, `driftYear` −1%): a well-served city doubles in about a decade.
- The calendar in the Career: research takes a month for every 12½ minutes a project used to take (`IC.techMonths`: 2 to 12 months); deals run 9–18 months (`IC.DEAL.months`), an offer comes every 1–2 months (sooner with a good name); loans run 1–4 years at 1% a month (`IC.LOANS`; the live clock keeps `IC.LOANS_LIVE`); the statement is monthly (`IC.monthStatement`) with a review each January (`IC.yearReview`); pavement wears out over its `IC.PAVE[m].life` months (asphalt 8 years, concrete 15), buildings over `IC.BUILDING_LIFE` (15 years), airliners are replaced at 20 years. Before the war the Minister's confidence settles towards 60 + 1.5 × (airline satisfaction − 58) over about `IC.CONF_MO` = 2 months, so it says how the sector is doing lately; goals and decisions push it and the push fades.
- Seasons (`IC.SEASON` in weather.js, Career only): winter fog (and fog forming from 01:00 to 11:00), snow (visibility 1.2 km: a landing system is needed) and 25–30% stronger winds; summer thunderstorms; leisure travel up to 30% busier in July and August and 15% at Christmas, business thinner in August.
- SAM reach at best height: short range 20 km, medium range 70 km, long range 160 km (`IC.REACH`, `IC.MUN[..].range`).
- Supply: convoys 150/120/90/70 km/h on motorway/main/local/access roads (military, escorted), 35 km/h on tracks; load and unload 90 s; stock by rail at 200 km/h after 5 min loading; the Central Depot starts with six truck companies in Quick war.
- The Career starts with ₭5,500M (`IC.CAREER_START`, the owner's ₭5.5 billion: a big national airport that becomes the income engine, the civil radars a large country's airways need, and room for mistakes) and a ₭1M/h grant (about ₭70M a month) that follows the Minister's confidence (full at 60, half at 10) and is halved in Act I's last chapter. Confidence cannot fall below 5 before Act III; from Act III a full day at zero replaces you.
- Act I chapters (calendar months, minimum / fallback): the national airport (no timer), the capital's airport 10 / 15, the airspace 10 / 14, light aircraft 8 / 11, a second city 15 / 20 (and it waits until a city's demand is there), the economy 14 / 16. The scripted player needs at least `IC.ACT1_MIN_MO` = 36 months (it takes about four and three-quarter years; the whole Career about ten). Act II's incidents come 5 to 36 months in, Act III's gray zone rises over 24–28 months; before the war the enemy's reconnaissance comes by the calendar too; Act IV's war runs on the live clock (about four months of the calendar for the scripted player).
- Deals (`IC.DEAL` in aviation.js): offers bring 1–2 aircraft (flag, budget, regional 2; cargo, foreign 1), one more at a name of 60; contracts of 2–3 game days on the live clock, 9–18 months in the Career; charges −10% to +20% of list (length ×1.25 to ×0.5), exclusivity +8%; an airline accepts up to 2–25% over list by demand and name. Needs: stands by size, gates (flag 60%, foreign 50% of its stands), hangar space for aircraft based here (flag 0.25, regional 0.2, low-cost 0.15 a aircraft), cargo handling, fuel, terminal room. A late flight (over 20 min) costs a quarter of the landing fee, a cancelled one more; a bad day is a quarter of flights late or cancelled, three end the deal. Broken: our name −12 (−8 if we end it) and compensation. Honoured: +3 to +8. In the Career an offer comes every one or two months at a name of 50, sooner the better the name.
- The network (`IC.NETWORK` in growth.js): a city asks for an airport when 9,000 of its people a day would fly and no airport is within 150 km, once the capital carries 2,500 passengers a day; jets from 15,000.
- The enemy (`IC.EPACE`): act 2 after 6–10 h of probing (16 h at most), the shock when 12 h of production is saved, three limited strikes have flown and the defence has won 8 h (or after 28 h of saving), a lull of 5–8 h, act 4 after 16 h of winning and 36 h of war (52 h at most); calms of 3–4 h in act 4. Shock: up to 12 ballistic and 20 cruise missiles over four targets. In `tools/enemy-timeline.js` with the scripted commander the Prime Minister slides from about 70 to 50–60 by the shock, which costs up to about 16, and ends the three days at 14–34.
- The Prime Minister loses at most `IC.PM_RAID_CAP` = 10 to what one raid breaks, on top of its after-action judgement, which counts for less when the raid was small (under 12 weapons).
- Quick war (the owner found it "woefully under-equipped"): it starts with ₭4,800M, a ₭300M/h defence grant (`IC.QW_GRANT`) and 75% of city and trade taxes (`IC.QW_TAX_SHARE`), about four times the earlier money. Already deployed: a long-range battery over the capital; medium-range and short-range layers over the main air base and the two largest cities; two 3D radars and a navigation jammer along the northern front; and a forward depot. Fourteen research items are done at the start (IADS, C-RAM, terminal BMD rounds, decoys, ECCM, ESM, counter-battery radar, type recognition, call-in teams, heavy trucks, rapid runway repair, better AAMs and countermeasures). The reserve holds more (`startingForces` in `state.js`). `econtest.js quick` prints the scripted commander of `qwplayer.js`: layers over the capital, the forward air base and the two largest cities within the first hour, radars seeing about 75% of the hostile border by the end of day 2, running costs close to income by then.
- Career grants by act: ₭1M/h, 4, 60 (the air defence the story hands over in Act III costs about ₭100M/h to run), 40, and the share of city taxes (`IC.STORY_TAX`): none, 1%, 15%, all (Acts II and III last years: 10% of the taxes over Act II was ₭2B a month); each act from II on starts with ₭300M, ₭900M, ₭1,000M.
- A radiating radar costs 15% more to keep for every other radar on its band within 70 km (where they would blind each other).
- Controllers: one area controller handles 10 flights at once, approach 7, tower 5 (+4 for an approach radar); a flight on an airway counts 0.6, one outside radar 1.5 times, climbing or descending 1.3, holding 1.5. Each controller costs ₭0.08M an hour (airport running costs). The country starts with one area sector of four. Overflights pay ₭0.5M on our airways, ₭0.25M off them.

## Owner's preferences

- The interface floats on the map, with no hard borders, and is readable (15 px base). Few menus: one inspector for the selection, war rooms for the rest.
- Realistic but explained. Punish bad design, and always show why.
- The player decides; the game does not script outcomes. Peacetime weapons are on Hold: shooting first is the player's choice.
- A slow burn: the game should start small (one airport, civil aviation) and grow in scope as the player learns.
